\subsection{Introduction}
In this section, we present our third contribution, which is the platform for human-swarm interaction. We present the setup and the software architecture that we have developed to control drones indoors around humans. We also present the results of the evaluation of the platform.

Our solution works on premise with a web-based interface to control the drones as it requires real-time computation. \textbf{Both for drone control and human pose detection computations have to be synchronized} as both subjects are in the same environment. The two systems are stateless allowing for a more flexible and scalable system. \textbf{They have to run on premise to keep them synchronized.} The drones are controlled using the \gls{DCK} and the human operator is localized using the \gls{HLK}. The two systems work together to ensure that the drones can navigate the environment safely and efficiently while interacting with the human operator. The main challenge is to have \textbf{the two systems work together in real-time} to ensure that the drones can avoid collisions with the human operator and other obstacles in the environment.

\subsection{Platform for Human-Swarm Interaction}


Our system is especially useful for testing new control strategies and algorithms in a safe and controlled environment. The platform provides a web-based interface for controlling the drones and visualizing their positions and orientations in real-time. The platform also provides a \gls{3D} visualization of the drones in the environment, allowing users to see the drones' positions and orientations in real-time. The platform is designed to be flexible and extensible, allowing for the addition of new features and functionalities as needed.
The web interface acts as a monitoring tool for the platform. See Figure \ref{figHSkeleton} for the positions of the elements of the platform through its web interface run for a few seconds.

The platform is composed of two main systems: the drone control kernel (\gls{DCK}) and the human localization kernel (\gls{HLK}). The DCK is responsible for controlling the drones and ensuring that they move safely and efficiently in the environment. The HLK is responsible for detecting the human operator's position and orientation in the environment and ensuring that the drones interact with the human operator in a safe and controlled manner. The two systems work together to ensure that the drones can navigate the environment safely and efficiently while interacting with the human operator. The main challenge is to have the two systems work together in real-time to ensure that the drones can avoid collisions with the human operator and other obstacles in the environment.

\subsection{Opportunitites}

We have successfully built a usable platform with known people and subjects. We plan on testing it further and giving it to students to test further and develop on.
Students can access the platform and develop their own algorithms to control the drones and have them interact with them in real-time. This will allow students to experiment with different control strategies and learn about the challenges of controlling drones in indoor environments especially without having to work on the hardware part of the drones.

One student Maximilien Menesguen \cite{max} developed the famous boids \cite{reynolds1987boids} algorithm in Unity to make the drones follow a moving point in space using our UDP server, a work in progress. 
One of the main challenges in controlling drones indoors around humans is ensuring that the drones can avoid collisions with people and objects. The system continuously monitors the surroundings and adjusts the simulated drones' trajectories to avoid collisions.
it is based on the original boids algorithm \cite{reynolds1987boids}. The boids algorithm is a simple and elegant model for simulating the flocking behavior of birds. It is based on three simple rules: separation, alignment, and cohesion. The separation rule ensures that the drones maintain a safe distance from each other, the alignment rule ensures that the drones move in the same direction, and the cohesion rule ensures that the drones stay together as a group. By following these rules, the drones can navigate the environment safely and efficiently.

Thelignment rule is to be modified to take into account the position of the human operator so the drones interact more with the human. Thus we plan on setting the red Ball of the UAVs to be the user. This modification allows the drones to follow the human operator while maintaining a safe distance from them. The cohesion rule has also been modified to ensure that the drones stay within a predefined area around the human operators. This last modification would allow the drones to move around the human operator without staying too far from them.

We plan to add the repulsion rule directly to the human operator and the drones. This rule would ensure that the drones maintain a safe distance from the human operator at all times. Adding the security layer to the platform is also a priority.
